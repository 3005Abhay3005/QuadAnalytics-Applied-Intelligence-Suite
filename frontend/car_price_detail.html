Introduction

The Used Car Price Estimator is a regression model that predicts the market resale value of a pre-owned vehicle based on key attributes. It addresses one of the core challenges in the automotive industry: the asymmetric information between buyers and sellers in the second-hand car market.

By encoding structured features — vehicle age, mileage, brand positioning, model series, powertrain capacity (CC & bhp), fuel technology, transmission type, and location — into a tuned Gradient Boosting Regressor, the model produces estimates that closely track real market transaction prices across major Indian metropolitan hubs.

Dataset Overview

The training data consists of 6,019 vehicle listings across Indian urban markets (Mumbai, Pune, Chennai, Delhi, Hyderabad, Bangalore, Kolkata, Jaipur, Coimbatore, Ahmedabad, Kochi). Each listing includes the car name, location, year of purchase, kilometers driven, fuel type, transmission, owner history, mileage, engine displacement, and power output.

Vehicle age is computed dynamically as the difference between the current year and the year of manufacture — converting a calendar year into an age feature that directly captures true depreciation duration. Specific power density (bhp/CC) and annual usage intensity (km/yr) were engineered to capture vehicle wear and efficiency.

Key Dataset Facts

  • 6,019 records across 30+ manufacturers and 200+ vehicle model series
  • Fuel types: Diesel (53%), Petrol (45%), CNG, LPG, Electric (2%)
  • Target variable: transaction price in Indian Rupees (Lakhs)
  • Top predictive features: Engine displacement, Power, Vehicle Age, Model Series
  • Training split: 80% train (4,815) / 20% test (1,204)

Machine Learning Methodology

The model uses a tuned Gradient Boosting Regressor wrapped in a TransformedTargetRegressor with logarithmic scaling (log1p). Categorical features (brand, model series, location, fuel type, transmission) are one-hot encoded, ordinal features (owner history) are rank encoded, and numeric features are scaled using RobustScaler to resist outlier distortions.

Gradient Boosting was chosen due to its ability to capture complex non-linear feature interactions — particularly between brand prestige, engine power density, and depreciation rates over time.

Training Pipeline

  • Preprocessing of unit strings (CC, bhp, kmpl) and missing value imputation
  • Engineered features: annual driving intensity and specific power density
  • Target log-transformation to stabilize right-skewed pricing distributions
  • Gradient Boosting Regressor with 650 estimators, learning rate 0.038, depth 5
  • Benchmarked using R² (90.56%), Mean Absolute Error (MAE: 1.27 Lakh INR), and RMSE
